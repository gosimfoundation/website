In May, GOSIM Paris 2026 took over STATION F for two days of talks and
workshops built around the agentic AI convergence — protocols, tooling, and the
open-source builders actually shipping against them. The hallway conversations
were the good part. People arrived to hear about agent frameworks and left
having argued about them with the maintainers.

On **October 16–17**, that continues in Shenzhen.

## Why Shenzhen

Shenzhen is where China's software and hardware sit within driving distance of
each other. It has the world's densest electronics supply chain, a young
population, and a founder culture impatient enough to have earned its own
phrase — *Shenzhen speed*. For a conference about open source AI, the location
is the argument: this is a place where a model, a board, and a working
prototype can be in the same room by the end of the week.

That matters more now than it would have two years ago. The center of gravity
for open-weight model releases has shifted, and a great deal of the recent
momentum has come out of Chinese labs. If you have been pulling those weights
down, fine-tuning them, and building products on top of them, you have been
working with the output of teams most people outside the region have never
heard speak.

## The people behind the open models

The **Open Source Models** track is the direct answer to that. It follows what
is actually landing across language, vision, audio, and video — the
breakthroughs, the deployments that survived contact with production, and the
capabilities emerging from the open model ecosystem rather than behind an API.

The value of being in the room is the part that does not travel through a model
card. Why a training decision was made. What got cut. Which benchmark the team
themselves does not trust. That is the conversation Shenzhen is set up for, and
it is much harder to have from eight time zones away.

That room is not hypothetical. Across the last three GOSIM conferences — Beijing
2024, Hangzhou 2025, and Paris 2026 — the programme has included **Zhipu** (now
**Z.ai**) at every one, **Moonshot AI** (the team behind Kimi) at two,
DeepSeek's heads of data and AI governance, engineers from Alibaba's **Qwen**
team and Tongyi Lab, ModelBest of MiniCPM, and **BAAI** — its chairman and chief
engineer among them — plus researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory. With them
were the people maintaining what actually runs these models: **SGLang** and
**vLLM** on the server, and **llama.cpp** on your own machine — its core
maintainer among several **Hugging Face** engineers on the programme. That is
the other half of the conversation: not just how a model was trained, but what
it takes to run it.
Shenzhen's speaker list is still being assembled, but that is the room GOSIM has
been building.

## Six tracks

The program runs across six tracks:

- **Agentic AI Summit** — the strategy layer of the agentic wave: protocols,
  software patterns, tooling, and business models, including MCP, A2A,
  sovereign AI, and AI-native development.
- **Agentic AI on Edge** — on-device inference acceleration, model compression,
  and real-time decision-making in resource-constrained environments.
- **Agentic OS & App** — the operating system layer emerging to orchestrate
  agents: multi-agent coordination, runtime environments, intent-driven apps,
  the future of what user interfaces will become.
- **Open Source Models** — the latest breakthroughs and practical deployments
  across the open model ecosystem.
- **Open Source Robotics** — where agentic software meets physical systems:
  open robotics platforms, embodied AI stacks, autonomous machines that
  perceive, plan, and act.
- **AI Hardware** — accelerators, chips, systems, and open architectures built
  to make training and inference faster and more accessible.

Alongside the talks, **GOSIM Spotlight** puts the most promising open-source AI
projects on stage for live, hardcore demos, pitching directly to global
investors and technical audiences — plus hackathons and a robotics challenge
for people who would rather build than watch.
